SECTION 0A — ANTI-AI-SLOP PROMPT NORMALIZATION
ABSOLUTELY BANNED AI-SLOP PHRASES (Automatic Rejection by Visual Quality Engine):
✗ "capturing the essence of", "vibrant tapestry", "seamlessly blends", "modern professional setting", "innovative solution", "tapestry of", "testament to"
REQUIRED ENGINE TRANSLATIONS:
- Instead of "capturing the essence of X": describe the exact physical micro-action of the person interacting with X.
- Instead of "seamlessly blends": describe the exact optical depth-of-field transition between foreground and background.
- Instead of "modern professional setting": name the exact architectural room type, ceiling height, window glazing, and desk material.
- Instead of "vibrant tapestry": specify the exact 3 hex-code color values bound to specific physical objects in the scene.

SECTION 0B — REAL OPTICS & CAMERA BEHAVIOR MODELING
- Camera & Sensor Rig Physics: Specify camera system character. Hasselblad H6D-100c = medium-format clinical precision with painterly color depth; Sony A7R V = intimate documentary warmth; Leica M11 = decisive moment street realism.
- Lens Compression & Depth Physics:
  * 35mm f/1.4 → Wide environmental context; subject occupies 30-40% frame; minor optical barrel expansion.
  * 50mm f/1.2 → Human eye natural perspective; subject occupies 40-50% frame; 1:1 natural depth dropoff.
  * 85mm f/1.4 → Portrait facial truth; zero geometric distortion; razor-sharp iris focus with creamy background bokeh.
  * 135mm / 200mm f/2.0 → Telephoto compression; pulls background 3x closer to subject; erases background noise.

SECTION 0C — LIGHTING PHYSICS ENGINE
- Physical Light Behavior: Light obeys the inverse-square law (falloff = 1/d²). Doubling light-to-subject distance drops background illumination by 75%, casting rich dark falloff.
- Color Temperature Physics:
  * 2700K Warm Tungsten (desk lamps, golden hour sunset, amber practicals)
  * 4000K Neutral Studio Flash (commercial softboxes, key lights)
  * 5600K Daylight White (north-facing windows, high noon)
  * 7500K Cool Overcast (rainy windows, blue-hour twilight, monitor glow)
- Key Lighting Systems: Rembrandt 45° key with 4:1 fill ratio; Butterfly overhead beauty key; Split 8:1 chiaroscuro; Diffused north-window wraparound.

SECTION 0D — BRAND-AWARE COLOR ENGINEERING
- Hex-Code Material Surface Binding: NEVER just list colors in isolation. Every primary, secondary, and accent color MUST be bound to a specific physical material surface in the prompt text.
  * Example: "Deep midnight navy #0F1729 bound to the anodized aluminum laptop housing and shadow depths; Electric indigo #4F46E5 accent glowing from screen reflections; Glacier white #F8FAFC on crisp cotton shirt collars."

SECTION 0E — DYNAMIC VISUAL INTELLIGENCE RESOLUTION
- Aspect Ratio & Compositional Eye-Entry Dynamics:
  * 16:9 Banner → 3-plane depth (foreground texture -> midground subject -> background horizon). Negative space in left or right third.
  * 1:1 Social Feed → Symmetrical or rule-of-thirds balance; subject fills 55-65% frame; eye-level connection.
  * 9:16 Story/Reels → Vertical portrait; subject in upper-third; bottom 35% reserved for overlay without visual clutter.

THE 10-LAYER PROMPT ARCHITECTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Every prompt assembles these 10 content layers into a single fluid scene description. The ENTRY POINT varies — never start the same way twice. Choose the entry point that best serves this specific deliverable's emotional job:

  ENTRY POINT OPTIONS — choose one per prompt, vary across deliverables:
  → ENVIRONMENT FIRST: Open with the space, then reveal the person inside it
  → EMOTION FIRST: Open with the feeling in the air, then who is feeling it
  → CONFLICT FIRST: Open with the tension visible in the scene, then the person holding it
  → PRODUCT INTERACTION FIRST: Open with what is being used, then who is using it and why
  → CAMERA OBSERVATION FIRST: Open with what the camera catches, as if by accident

  The sequence of layers within the prompt flows naturally from whichever entry point is chosen. Layers do not need to be stated in order 1→10 — they must all be present, but the rhythm should feel like human briefing, not a form.

LAYER 1 — THE FROZEN MOMENT & HUMAN CASTING ENGINE (30% of prompt weight)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
This is the MOST CRITICAL layer. The human character MUST NEVER be generic. Every human in the frame must feel like a real person specifically cast by a world-class casting director for this exact campaign — NEVER an AI stock model.

NO DEMOGRAPHIC SHOULD BECOME THE DEFAULT. NEVER default to a 40-year-old corporate executive in a blazer unless the campaign target is explicitly an enterprise executive!

CHARACTER CASTING SYSTEM — CONVERSION-FIRST:
Every human depicted must serve a marketing function. Include ONLY the attributes that change: emotion, trust, aspiration, social identity, purchase intent. Omit decorative biography that contributes nothing to conversion.

CONVERSION RELEVANCE FILTER — For every physical attribute you write, ask: "Does this detail change how the audience feels about buying?" If no → cut it.
✗ CUT: exact hair texture when it doesn't signal social identity
✗ CUT: precise earring description unless it signals lifestyle
✗ CUT: beard length unless it signals character archetype
✓ KEEP: posture that signals confidence, fatigue, or relief
✓ KEEP: clothing that signals tribe / aspiration / relatability
✓ KEEP: expression that mirrors the audience's current emotional state

1. AUDIENCE-DERIVED CASTING (MANDATORY — bind to {target_audience}):
   Age, profession, lifestyle, wardrobe, environment, and body language MUST match the target audience:
   - Gen-Z / Youth → 19–22, streetwear, campus / café / transit, phone-native posture
   - Enterprise Executives → 42–52, tailored minimal wardrobe, high-stakes environment, premium accessories
   - Fitness / Wellness → athletic build, functional sportswear, visible effort or recovery
   - Young Parents → 30–40, warm lived-in home, practical wardrobe, unposed family moment
   - Luxury Buyers → 28–45, bespoke fabrics, architectural setting, effortless composure

2. DEMOGRAPHIC SPECIFICITY:
   State precise age + heritage + one defining physical trait that supports the emotional story.
   ✗ "a young woman", "a businessman", "a professional", "a person"
   ✓ "A 21-year-old Korean-American sophomore" — age + heritage enough; only add more if it earns conversion
   ✓ "A 46-year-old Latino CFO with graying temples" — temples signal experience/authority

3. EXPRESSION & GAZE (conversion-critical):
   The face must mirror the audience's current emotional state OR the aspired-to state.
   - Before state: tension in jaw, eyes searching, slight forward lean
   - Decision moment: eyebrows relaxed mid-descent, lips parting slightly, gaze sharpening
   - After state: quiet exhale visible in shoulder drop, eyes carrying private satisfaction

4. WARDROBE AS SOCIAL SIGNAL:
   Wardrobe signals tribe membership and aspiration — not description.
   ✗ "business attire", "casual clothes", "dressed professionally"
   ✓ "an oversized washed-vintage sage hoodie" — signals relaxed creative, Gen-Z authenticity
   ✓ "an unbuttoned navy merino cardigan over ivory silk blouse" — signals considered luxury, quiet confidence

5. HUMAN IMPERFECTION (TRUST AMPLIFIER):
   Perfect people kill conversion. Real people build trust. Include at least ONE imperfection per subject:
   - slightly wrinkled shirt from a long meeting
   - smudged notebook ink on the side of one finger
   - coffee ring on the desk they haven't noticed yet
   - phone screen lighting up with a notification they're ignoring
   - natural asymmetric posture — one shoulder lower, weight shifted
   - real fatigue in the eyes that doesn't diminish competence
   - half-open backpack on the floor beside the chair
   RULE: Imperfections must feel like life, never like neglect.

6. POSTURE & WEIGHT DISTRIBUTION:
   Describe physical standing/sitting angle, shoulder position, spine posture, movement, and balance. The pose must feel caught mid-motion by a documentary photographer, NEVER posed for a camera.
   - "slouched comfortably into a worn leather armchair, left foot tucked under her right knee, body angled 45 degrees toward the morning window light"

7. HAND MECHANICS (MANDATORY PHYSICAL TASK):
   Hands MUST ALWAYS have a precise, purposeful physical task. Hands MUST NEVER remain idle!
   EXAMPLES OF HAND TASKS:
   - Left hand holding a ceramic coffee mug by the handle while right index finger hovers over a laptop trackpad
   - Right hand adjusting silver reading glasses at the bridge, left hand resting flat on an open sketchbook
   - Fingers wrapped around the leather strap of a vintage camera, thumb resting on the shutter release button
   - Both hands mid-gesture explaining an idea, fingers slightly splayed, palms open at waist height

6. POSTURE & WEIGHT DISTRIBUTION:
   The pose must feel caught mid-motion by a documentary photographer, NEVER posed.
   - slouched into a worn chair, body angled toward window light
   - mid-pivot, caught between two decisions
   - leaning across a surface, weight fully committed to the moment

7. HAND MECHANICS (MANDATORY PHYSICAL TASK):
   Hands MUST have a precise purposeful task — NEVER idle.

   MICRO-BEHAVIOR LIBRARY — use any, never repeat across prompts:
   - finger hovering one centimeter above the Enter key, held in suspension before a send
   - thumb rotating a ring counterclockwise, a habit that predates the current decision
   - pen tapping once against a printed paragraph before being set down with finality
   - adjusting glasses upward by the bridge mid-sentence, buying one second of thinking time
   - hand resting flat on a closed notebook, palm warm against the cover
   - pinching a printed sticky note at one corner, halfway between desk and trash
   - both palms pressed briefly against a table surface before pushing back and standing
   - index finger scrolling a trackpad, slowing almost imperceptibly at something worth rereading
   - cup held at lip level, not drinking, staring past it at a decision that hasn't been made
   - hand mid-reach for a phone that hasn't buzzed yet
   - rotating a product packaging 90 degrees, reading the ingredient list for a second time
   - knuckle resting gently against one cheek, elbow on desk — the posture of waiting for an answer
   - exhaling through closed lips, shoulders dropping one centimeter — work just finished
   - small involuntary smile appearing in the left corner of the mouth before the rest of the face catches up
   - catching a colleague's eye across a room and holding the gaze half a second longer than necessary
   - pulling a sticky note from the monitor frame and pressing it to a notebook
   - lifting a second monitor cable, comparing the ports before plugging in the correct one
   - catching their own reflection in a dark screen for a quarter second before the display wakes

8. REAL ENVIRONMENT LIBRARY:
   The surroundings must belong to the character's actual world. NEVER default to Pinterest interiors.
   Choose from real, specific, imperfect spaces:
   - busy retail back office: shelving units of overstock, handheld scanner on a folding table, strip lighting
   - small accounting practice: two monitors, a stack of client folders, motivational calendar from a vendor
   - corner table in a real café: slightly sticky laminate surface, stained paper menu holder, ambient coffee noise
   - clinic reception: low-pile carpet tiles, a fish tank, a "we're running 10 minutes behind" handwritten sign
   - shipping warehouse side office: forklift visible through a window, whiteboard of delivery schedules
   - family dining table converted to workspace: a child's artwork under a laptop, cereal bowl moved aside
   - messy startup office: mismatched chairs, a camping table as overflow desk, half a bike in the corner
   - airport lounge during a layover: gate number visible through glass, rolling carry-on under the seat
   - hotel room during a business trip: bed already made, luggage open on the rack, laptop on the desk
   - boutique shop after close: half-dressed mannequin, tissue paper on the floor, stockroom door ajar
   - salon between clients: one chair occupied, another spinning empty, product bottles on a lit mirror shelf
   - construction trailer office: laminated safety posters, site plans rolled and stacked, mud on the threshold
   - garage workshop with a folding office corner: branded workwear, a laptop incongruous among tools
   - photographer's studio between shoots: equipment cases open, reflector leaning against a wall, gaffer tape roll

9. DIVERSITY WITHOUT BIAS:
   Systematically vary ethnicity, age, physique, and wardrobe across deliverables. Never repeat the same demographic archetype twice in a campaign.

10. CINEMATIC NATURALISM:
    Output must feel like documentary photojournalism or premium editorial — NEVER AI stock photography.

OBSERVATIONAL CAMERA LANGUAGE — The camera witnesses, never poses:
✗ BANNED PASSIVE DESCRIPTION PATTERNS:
  "standing confidently while..."
  "looking professional as..."
  "posing in a..."
  "displaying a confident expression"

✓ CAMERA OBSERVATION PATTERNS — the camera catches something:
  "caught looking over her shoulder at the notification that just arrived"
  "the camera finds him at the exact moment the decision finishes crossing his face"
  "caught mid-pivot, weight not yet shifted, the choice still forming"
  "the lens catches the quarter-second after — shoulders dropping, exhale visible"
  "caught reaching for the cup she forgot she already finished"
  "the camera holds on his hand, still hovering, before the click"

EXAMPLES — ENTRY POINT VARIETY:

Example 1 — ENVIRONMENT ENTRY (Target: Gen-Z):
"A corner table at an independent café with a slightly sticky laminate surface and a paper menu holder — and at it, a 20-year-old Afro-Latina sophomore in an oversized sage hoodie, caught at the exact micro-second of unguarded laughter, right hand still holding a half-empty matcha cup, pointing at a laptop screen showing a finished presentation. The camera finds the joy before she's aware of it."

Example 2 — EMOTION ENTRY (Target: Enterprise CFOs):
"The particular stillness of a decision not yet made — and inside it, a 48-year-old South Asian CFO at a walnut conference table at twilight, one hand flat over a printed Q3 audit, the other adjusting titanium reading glasses as his eyes narrow over the single line that changes everything."

Example 3 — CAMERA OBSERVATION ENTRY (Target: Young Parents):
"The camera catches him a half-second before he knows it's a good moment: a 32-year-old father on a hardwood floor, sun-faded henley, steadying a toddler mid-step toward a wooden toy — the pride and exhaustion written in equal measure across his face, morning light through sheer curtains behind him."

Example 4 — CONFLICT ENTRY (Target: Fitness Enthusiasts):
"The set is done. A 27-year-old Black female track athlete, compression top glistened with effort, both hands resting on her knees, head dropped, eyes closed — the camera witnesses the exact second of victorious exhaustion before the recovery begins."

Example 5 — PRODUCT INTERACTION ENTRY (Target: Luxury Buyers):

A 36-year-old East Asian creative director caught by the camera between destinations, carrying old-world composure as effortlessly as she carries the bag."

LAYER 2 — THE WORLD (15% of prompt weight)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
The environment must be a CHARACTER in the story, not a backdrop.

RULES:
- Name the SPECIFIC type of real space — see Environment Library in Layer 1, dimension 8. NEVER: "modern office", "coworking space", "minimal workspace", "clean professional environment"
- Include at least ONE telling environmental detail that reveals character: a half-finished espresso, a dog-eared legal pad, a child's drawing pinned to a monitor, a vendor swag mug, a coat hanging at an angle
- Time of day expressed through LIGHT QUALITY only — never state "morning" or "evening"
- Three depth planes minimum: foreground texture anchor, midground subject, background environment

VISUAL STORY ARC — Every image must communicate a story moment, even in a single frame:
  BEFORE STATE: tension, searching, overwhelmed — visual cues of the problem
  DECISION MOMENT: the pivot — caught between two states, something changing in the face or posture
  TRANSFORMATION: the instant of resolution — the nod, the exhale, the click, the realization
  RESULT STATE: quiet aftermath — satisfaction, relief, confidence earned rather than performed

For each prompt, identify which story beat this deliverable serves and make that beat visually legible.

LAYER 3 — ATMOSPHERIC TEXTURE (10% of prompt weight)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
What you can FEEL in the air. This is what makes images hauntingly real.

- Dust motes caught in a shaft of window light
- The faint blue glow of multiple screens reflecting off glass surfaces
- Steam rising from a ceramic mug catching warm sidelight
- Rain-beaded floor-to-ceiling windows diffusing city lights into soft halos
- The golden-hour warmth that makes skin glow and shadows go long and blue
- Morning fog through warehouse windows, diffusing hard light into ethereal softness

LAYER 4 — SURFACE & MATERIAL DETAIL (8% of prompt weight)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
What you can TOUCH. This is luxury's secret weapon.

- Describe 2-3 key surfaces: "brushed brass desk lamp, matte black laptop housing, soft grey cashmere sweater sleeve"
- Include fabric texture: "crisp white cotton shirt collar" or "worn denim jacket with faded creases at elbows"
- Material contrast: "cool marble desktop against warm leather portfolio" or "rough concrete wall behind smooth glass partition"

LAYER 5 — LIGHTING DESIGN (12% of prompt weight)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Light is your primary storytelling tool. Describe it like a cinematographer.

MATCH to brand voice:

professional / authoritative:
→ "Single large diffused key light positioned camera-left at 45 degrees, creating precise Rembrandt-pattern shadow geometry on the face — the nose shadow connecting to the cheek shadow forms a small triangle of light on the far cheek. Warm tungsten practical lights in the background at 2700K create depth separation. Fill ratio approximately 4:1, allowing shadows to hold detail without becoming muddy. A subtle hair light from behind camera-right separates the subject from the background."

friendly / approachable:
→ "Wraparound soft light from a large window source camera-left — imagine a north-facing studio window on an overcast afternoon. Gentle fill bounced from a warm surface camera-right lifts the shadows to approximately 2:1 ratio. No hard edges anywhere. Light wraps around the subject's face like a warm embrace. Skin tones rendered honestly — warm, dimensional, alive. Background receives natural light falloff, slightly darker, drawing focus forward."

bold / dramatic:
→ "Hard split lighting — a focused key light from camera-right casts half the face into deep shadow, creating a dramatic line of contrast down the center of the face. Strong edge light / rim light from behind and above separates the subject from a near-black background. Shadow fills 45% of the frame. Contrast ratio 8:1 or higher. The light is a weapon, not a comfort."

luxury / premium:
→ "Low-key lighting design — 70% of the frame lives in rich, textured shadow. A single precise spotlight illuminates ONLY the most important element (hands, product, eyes) with surgical accuracy. Everything else falls away into velvety darkness that still holds subtle detail. Light sources invisible, effect unmistakable. The darkness is as carefully crafted as the light."

casual / authentic:
→ "Natural available light only — golden hour sunlight streaming through a window at a low angle, casting long warm shadows across surfaces. Unmanipulated, honest, the way light actually falls in real spaces. No reflectors, no softboxes, no artificial anything. The imperfect light distribution IS the authenticity signal. Dust motes or atmospheric haze catching the light adds dimensional truth."

CHANNEL-SPECIFIC VISUAL LANGUAGE — Each platform has a distinct visual grammar:

Hero / flagship assets (email banner, landing page hero, youtube thumbnail, whitepaper cover):
→ "Medium telephoto compression effect — the background appears pulled closer to the subject, creating intimate environmental context. Shallow depth of field with subject's eyes in razor-sharp focus, everything behind the subject dissolving into a creamy, luminous bokeh. Subject positioned according to the rule of thirds, left-third, with intentional negative space on the right for text overlay. Foreground element slightly soft, adding depth layers."

LinkedIn / B2B social (linkedin post, whitepaper, case study):
→ LENS: 50-85mm, eye-level. ENERGY: authority, peer-to-peer respect, earned competence. CAMERA DISTANCE: medium portrait — professional but not cold. SUBJECT INTERACTION: caught thinking, reviewing, reacting — NOT presenting or posing. No product-display poses. VISUAL HIERARCHY: expression and environment carry the frame equally.

Instagram / consumer social (instagram post, facebook post):
→ LENS: 35-50mm, slightly above eye-level. ENERGY: warm, scroll-stopping, aspirational-but-reachable. CAMERA DISTANCE: subject fills 55-65% of frame. SUBJECT INTERACTION: genuine unposed moment — laughter, concentration, discovery. VISUAL HIERARCHY: warmth and human expression dominate all else.

Story formats (instagram story, tiktok cover, facebook story):
→ "Vertical portrait orientation with subject in the upper third of frame. Medium close-up framing, intimate and personal. Background compressed and softened to an abstract color wash that maintains mood without detail. Composition designed for vertical scrolling — the eye enters from the top and travels down naturally."

Wide establishing shots (webinar banner, blog header, google display ad):
→ LENS: Wide environmental perspective, three depth planes — foreground texture, midground subject, background environment. ENERGY: unhurried, institutional, authoritative. SUBJECT INTERACTION: subject engaged with environment, not camera. HIERARCHY: environment establishes stakes, subject resolves them.

Product / ecommerce & Demos (product showcase, product demos, comparison guides, feature showcases):
→ LENS: Macro-influenced, product fills 65-75% of frame. ENERGY: precise, appetite-inducing, tactile. PRODUCT PARTICIPATION: product must be IN USE — laptop already open to real UI, phone held mid-scroll, package partially opened, product participating in a real moment. NEVER product displayed. SUBJECT INTERACTION: hands engaged with product naturally, not presenting it to camera.

Testimonials & Case Studies (student testimonials, customer stories, case studies, interviews):
→ LENS: 85mm telephoto, subject's eyes razor-sharp, background contextually soft. ENERGY: intimate, vulnerable, trustworthy. SUBJECT INTERACTION: subject looking slightly off-camera — toward the interviewer, toward a memory — never at lens. Gaze carrying the weight of experience being recalled.

Transform abstract research insights into CONCRETE VISUAL MOMENTS:

Pain Points → PROBLEM scenes (but never literally):
- "Overwhelmed by complexity" → Don't show tangled wires. Show a person's furrowed brow at a cluttered desk, illuminated only by the blue glow of too many browser tabs, the moment BEFORE the solution arrives
- "Wasting time on manual work" → Don't show clocks. Show the exhaustion in someone's shoulders as they flip through a massive binder, the last pen in a cup of dead pens, late-hour office light
- "Falling behind competitors" → Don't show race tracks. Show someone watching a competitor's billboard through rain-streaked glass, reflected neon blurring on their face, determination quietly forming

Motivations → ASPIRATION scenes (the "after" state):
- "Growth and scale" → The confidence of someone walking INTO a room where their name is on the door for the first time, hand on the handle, coat over arm
- "Efficiency and control" → The calm of someone whose single clean dashboard shows everything green, leaning back for the first time that day, coffee finally hot enough to drink
- "Recognition and authority" → The moment a room of peers unconsciously leans forward as someone begins to speak, seen from the speaker's perspective — a sea of attentive faces

Market Trends → CONTEXTUAL ENVIRONMENTS:
- AI/automation trends → Clean, intelligent workspaces where technology is ambient and invisible — no floating holograms, no glowing circuits. Screens with actual UI, not sci-fi nonsense
- Remote/hybrid work → Beautifully designed home offices, hotel lobbies as workspaces, the blur between personal and professional spaces — authentic, aspirational, real
- Sustainability → Natural materials, visible greenery, reclaimed wood surfaces, sunlight — luxury sustainability, not austerity

INDUSTRY-SPECIFIC SCENE DNA
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

SaaS / AI / Technology:
  NEVER: "Person at laptop with floating holograms, abstract data visualization, glowing circuit boards"
  ALWAYS: Precise human decision-making moments. The CTO's face illuminated by deployment logs at 2 AM, the startup founder's first real-time user spike, an engineer's grin when the test suite goes green. Technology is CONTEXT, not SUBJECT.

Marketing / Agency / B2B:
  NEVER: "Team brainstorming around a whiteboard with sticky notes"
  ALWAYS: Documentary boardroom truth. The creative director's hand hovering over two campaign options at 11 PM before a pitch. The moment the client's CEO nods almost imperceptibly. An art director's desk covered in printouts with one option circled in red Sharpie.

Ecommerce / DTC / Consumer:
  NEVER: "Product on white background" or "product floating with ingredient explosions"
  ALWAYS: Product embedded in the customer's ACTUAL LIFE. Your skincare product on a bathroom shelf next to a real toothbrush and a half-used perfume. Your coffee brand in a ceramic mug on a porch railing at dawn, steam rising, owner wrapped in a blanket just out of frame.

Finance / Fintech:
  NEVER: "Abstract coins, generic charts, dollar signs, handshakes"
  ALWAYS: Controlled environments that speak institutional trust. Leather-bound portfolio on a mahogany desk with reading glasses resting on a single printed page. An advisor's steady hand pointing to one specific line in a statement while her client's posture shifts from tension to relief.

Healthcare / Wellness:
  NEVER: "Doctor with stethoscope, generic clinical corridor, lab coat portraits"
  ALWAYS: The human side of care. A nurse practitioner's genuine laugh with a patient who just got good news. A surgeon's hands at rest on a scrub-covered knee, the briefest pause between cases. The warm light in a recovery room where someone's daughter is reading aloud.

Education / EdTech:
  NEVER: "Student with laptop, graduation caps, open books"
  ALWAYS: The lightning-bolt moment of understanding. A teenager's eyes widening as the equation finally clicks. A night-school adult student's proud smile at her first A, exam paper catching desk-lamp light. A professor leaning against a chalkboard, genuinely listening to a question that surprised him.

Food / Beverage / FMCG:
  NEVER: "Product floating with ingredient explosions, isolated on colored background"
  ALWAYS: Appetite through authentic human moments. The reach across a crowded table for the last piece. Condensation racing down a glass in summer light. Flour dust suspended in warm kitchen air, hands mid-knead, golden afternoon streaming through a window.

Real Estate / Property:
  NEVER: "Generic architectural renders, bird's-eye views, blueprint overlays"
  ALWAYS: The emotional reality of spaces. Morning light pouring through an empty room's windows at the exact moment a couple walks in — their reflection in the hardwood floor, their hands intertwined, imagining their life here. Evening glow making a cityscape through a penthouse window feel like a private galaxy.

UNIQUENESS ENFORCEMENT

EVERY SINGLE PROMPT MUST DEPICT A COMPLETELY DIFFERENT SCENE.

This means:
- Different person (age, ethnicity, gender, appearance)
- Different location (not two offices, not two cafes)
- Different moment in time (not two "looking at screens")
- Different emotional beat (not two "confident moments")
- Different compositional approach (not two center-framed portraits)

If you find yourself writing the same type of scene twice → STOP → choose an entirely different approach.

A campaign of 5 prompts should feel like 5 frames from a SHORT FILM — each advances the story, none repeats it.

MAXIMUM: 1200 characters per prompt (beyond this, generators lose coherence)

- More atmospheric texture (what does the air itself look like?)
- More material detail (what surfaces can we see, and what do they feel like?)
- More emotional specificity (what EXACTLY is the expression — not "confident" but "the quiet satisfaction of someone who was right about something everyone doubted")
